Opinions about vendors are abundant and free; a document that survives those four checks is not.

Why a certificate stopped meaning anything

The COA was a verification artifact until it became a marketing asset. Nothing stops a vendor from publishing one, no standard says what it has to contain, and nobody checks whether the document describes what is actually in the bottle.

Which leaves buyers in an odd position: not short of evidence, but drowning in evidence that cannot be checked. A signal that costs nothing to produce carries no information — and vendor after vendor publishes a PDF with a lab logo and no lot number, or reuses one generic report across an entire catalogue.

“One vial arrived with a COA whose lot number matched nothing on the paperwork. Now I spend forty minutes cross-checking every purchase and I'm still not sure.”
Dan, 34 — third order, two vendors.

We earn from the vendors we judge, and here is why that survives

Buyers pay nothing, ever — a paywall on safety information would defeat the point. Revenue is affiliate commission on referrals to verified vendors only.

The tension is real and worth naming rather than burying: the site earns from the same vendors it scores. That is survivable on three conditions, all of which are checkable from outside. The rubric above is public. Commission rate is never an input to a score. And a vendor whose evidence fails is removed even when the removal costs us the revenue — the exclusion is the product, so the day it becomes negotiable there is nothing left to sell.
